## Overview

Satoyama forests have been shaped through long-term interactions between people and forest ecosystems.

Changes in forest use, management practices, population, and local economies can influence biodiversity, resource availability, and community relationships with forests.

This project examines the ecological and social dimensions of satoyama management.

## Objectives

- Understand relationships between people and satoyama forests
- Evaluate the effects of forest management on biodiversity
- Examine local knowledge and forest-resource use
- Identify factors supporting continued community participation
- Contribute to sustainable satoyama management

## Research Activities

The project combines ecological investigation with social and community-based research.

Research activities include:

- Forest and biodiversity surveys
- Investigation of fungal and other biological communities
- Interviews with residents and forest users
- Documentation of local forest knowledge
- Analysis of management practices
- Evaluation of community participation

## Methods

The project uses methods such as:

- Field surveys
- Biodiversity assessment
- Ecological monitoring
- Interviews and questionnaires
- Participatory research
- Qualitative and quantitative analysis

## Expected Outcomes

This research aims to contribute to:

- Sustainable satoyama management
- Biodiversity conservation
- Maintenance of local forest knowledge
- Community-based forest activities
- Improved understanding of human–forest relationships
- Integration of ecological and social perspectives
