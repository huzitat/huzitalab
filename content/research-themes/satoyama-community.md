## Overview

Satoyama landscapes have been shaped through long-term interactions between people and nature.

Changes in population, lifestyles, and forest use have weakened many of these relationships. Our laboratory studies ways to reconnect people with forests through biodiversity, local knowledge, forest products, cultural practices, and community activities.

## Research Questions

- How have relationships between people and satoyama changed?
- How can local communities participate in forest management?
- What ecological and cultural values are maintained through human activity?
- How can forest products and local traditions support renewed engagement with forests?

## Research Topics

- Satoyama management
- Community participation
- Biodiversity and fungal communities
- Matsutake and forest environments
- Local knowledge and culture
- Environmental education

## Methods

We combine field surveys, fungal and DNA analysis, questionnaires, interviews, participatory observation, and collaborative research with local communities.
